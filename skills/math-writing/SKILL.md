---
name: math-writing
description: Use when writing or revising mathematical exposition for readers, including proofs, derivations, and passages with defined notation.
---

When a page proves something, first state everything it will prove, in formulas, before any supporting derivation. Give the assumptions and quantifiers needed to read it. An ordinary sentence and a display usually suffice.

Use familiar facts at the step that needs them. Explain why the decisive inference holds.

Name a quantity only when the name is used again or makes a calculation easier to read; otherwise write the expression. Do not define standard notation. When revising, remove aliases that fail this test. Keep proof-only names inside the proof.
