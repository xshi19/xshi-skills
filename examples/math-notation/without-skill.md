Let $X$ and $Y$ be real-valued random variables with $\mathbb{E}|X|<\infty$ and $\mathbb{E}|Y|<\infty$. Then $|\mathbb{E}[X]|\le\mathbb{E}|X|$ and $|\mathbb{E}[Y]|\le\mathbb{E}|Y|$, and the triangle inequality yields
$$
|\mathbb{E}[X]-\mathbb{E}[Y]|\le|\mathbb{E}[X]|+|\mathbb{E}[Y]|\le\mathbb{E}|X|+\mathbb{E}|Y|.
$$

Let $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ be a nonconstant real sample with mean $\bar{x}$ and standard deviation $s>0$. The standardized values $z_i=(x_i-\bar{x})/s$ satisfy
$$
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n z_i=\frac{1}{ns}\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-\bar{x})=0.
$$
